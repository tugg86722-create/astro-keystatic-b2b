Most Klaviyo accounts are not underperforming because the platform is wrong. They are underperforming because four or five flows were never built, and the two that were built have been live for two years without a single review.

Here are the nine flows I implement in every engagement, in priority order.

---

## Tier 1 — Revenue Recovery

### 1. Welcome Series (3–4 emails)

**Trigger:** new subscriber, no purchase in 30 days
**Structure:** brand story → proof → product category → offer
**Benchmark:** 8–14% of email-attributed revenue on established brands; higher on new ones

The welcome series is the only flow that gets opened by people who have never heard of you. It should not be a discount blast. The discount comes in email three, if at all.

**The most common mistake:** sending the discount first. You have just trained a brand-new subscriber to wait for a discount, and every subsequent email is now filtered into the promotions tab.

### 2. Browse Abandonment (2 emails)

**Trigger:** product viewed, no cart, no purchase
**Structure:** the exact product → proof/social proof → help + offer
**Benchmark:** 1.5–3% of abandoned product views convert

Timing matters more than copy here. Email one within 45 minutes, email two at 20–24 hours. After 24 hours the intent has largely evaporated.

### 3. High-Ticket Cart Recovery (2–3 emails)

**Trigger:** cart or checkout started, AOV above your store median
**Structure:** reminder with saved cart → reassurance (returns, shipping, guarantee) → human help

**This is a different flow from generic cart recovery and it is where high-ticket revenue actually sits.** A $180 abandoned cart needs reassurance, not a 10% code. Discounting a high-ticket cart teaches customers to abandon deliberately, and it destroys your margin on the exact segment you most want to keep.

Benchmark: 8–15% recovery on high-ticket carts *without* a discount code, versus 4–6% with one.

## Tier 2 — Retention & LTV

### 4. Post-Purchase Flow (3 emails)

**Trigger:** order fulfilled
**Structure:** confirmation + what happens next → care/use guide → replenishment or cross-sell

The second email is where most stores send a discount code immediately. Stop. The customer just paid full price. Send them the care guide, the sizing reference, the tracking detail, and then — 14 days later — a replenishment reminder for a consumable.

Post-purchase flow revenue is 2–4× higher than any promotional email, and it is the cheapest revenue you own because you already spent the acquisition cost.

### 5. Browse Abandonment → VIP Segmentation

**Trigger:** 3+ product views in 14 days, no purchase
**Structure:** curated edit of what they looked at → social proof → early access

Not every browser is a customer. Browsing three or four times without buying is a buying signal, and most stores have no flow that treats it as one.

### 6. Win-Back / VIP Reactivation (2–3 emails)

**Trigger:** no purchase in 90–180 days, high historic value
**Structure:** what is new since they left → an actual reason to return → a closing gesture

### 7. Replenishment / Reorder

**Trigger:** expected consumption cycle reached (supplements, beauty, consumables)
**Structure:** restock reminder → one-click reorder → subscription option

For consumable brands this flow alone is frequently worth more than the entire promotional calendar.

---

## Tier 3 — Protection & Deflection

### 8. Shipping Delay & Backorder Apology

**Trigger:** order marked delayed, or fulfilment SLA breached
**Structure:** proactive apology + the new date + a gesture

**This is the flow that prevents chargebacks.** Late delivery is the leading cause of disputes in apparel and home goods. A proactive apology with a new date converts a future dispute into a tolerated inconvenience. Silence converts it into a chargeback.

### 9. First-Order Review Request

**Trigger:** delivery confirmed + 7–10 days
**Structure:** how the product performed for you → review request → loyalty/referral nudge

---

## The Two Flows Almost Nobody Builds

If you build nothing else from this list, build these two.

**Shipping Delay Apology.** Because a chargeback costs you the order value, the payment fee, the shipping, the support time, and — at scale — a card network relationship that takes years to rebuild. One flow prevents a category of loss that no amount of CRO can recover.

**High-Ticket Cart Recovery without a discount.** Because your highest-value customers are being trained to abandon, and the habit shows up in your margin, not just your revenue.

---

## The Review Every 90 Days

A flow that was live for two years without review is not a flow, it is a liability. Every quarter, check for each flow:

- **Trigger still fires correctly** (product, collection or SKU references often break silently after a theme change)
- **Revenue attribution still matches reality**
- **The copy still sounds like the brand you run now**
- **Suppression logic still excludes purchasers, VIPs and unsubscribes correctly**
- **Rendering still works** — a Klaviyo template that broke during a Liquid change loses revenue invisibly

The single most common finding: a flow whose trigger points to a product handle that no longer exists, quietly sending nothing since the catalogue was reorganised six months ago.

---

## What Good Looks Like

A mature Klaviyo program on a high-ticket DTC brand should be generating **15%+ of total revenue** from automated flows. Not from campaigns. Flows.

If your flows are under 10% of revenue, the account is not under-optimised — it is under-built. That is a two-week implementation, not a three-month strategy engagement, and it is the cheapest revenue available to you.
